It’s 4:45 PM on a Friday as she finishes the final tasks to prepare for a new week before a much needed two day break, here comes Mr. Important with a brand new issue. She is accustomed to living a life of excellence, excelling, in everything she does and touches but with each gray hair, she’s learning and gaining wisdom. She understands that time and energy should be dispersed, equally amongst the tasks of the day and the replenishment of her. Energy is no longer drained, taken, stolen or given without having enough.

So Mr. Important gets acknowledged and placed into the list of to do’s for the next week. He’ll be alright! *Hell*, it was *His* idea to break down the days in the 24 measly little hours giving us just enough time to eat, sleep, bathe and work. That had to be a man’s idea. A divine feminine has so many other right brain activities to tend to. She governs the spiritual realm and nurtures the offspring of generations. Timelines are bent and several dimensions are experienced within a human’s day.

So where did the hustle culture even come from, that idea that to do more you would receive more validation? But at what cost to herself? What will she lose in the journey to gain and what will she really gain as she loses validation, of what exactly? The way I see it and the way I’ve experienced it, is it’s a piece of self lost when all of the emphasis is placed on material matters. When her spiritual house is in order, all is manifest in the physical and present for her needs. Everything begins in the spiritual realm before it can be manifested into reality.

![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/fb257704-7d9b-4191-abdf-c9a2d42e9634_1024x1536.png)

So how does she get from hustle culture to a soft life? Before we can break this down let’s understand the divine feminine a little deeper. When we talk about the Divine Feminine in African diasporic and indigenous spirituality, we are honoring the sacred power of feminine energy that has *Always* been at the heart of our traditions. This isn’t just about gender but I’m talking about the Yeye’s the Iya’s the grandmothers, mama’s, Sistar’s and aunties. The womb holders and the flame keepers of this sacred role. It’s about life force, creation, intuition, and deep ancestral wisdom. Our goddesses, our ancestral mamas, our powerful feminine deities that represent fertility, protection, healing, and the kind of nurturing that births nations and revives generations.

This is the energy that knows how to create life and also how to protect it. It’s soft and fierce. It’s intuitive and wise. She is the archetype of love that don’t play about hers. It’s the ancestral remembrance reminding us that we come from power and that we are meant to live in that truth.

So with this being said, it’s OK to be strong and soft at the same time. It’s okay to be excellent but not exhausted. It’s okay to give and receive and most importantly it’s OK to say no. For too long daughters of the Muurs, and women of the African diaspora have been expected to take on the weight of the world on her shoulders with very little reciprocation and the least amount of recognition. Always expected to do more and receive less and some even have the audacity to think we should be grateful for what we get. My how the story got twisted upside down!

![](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/fbef0de7-11af-47f1-a956-684e43ec2243_1024x1536.png)

She is reclaiming her power, not because it was ever lost, but because it had been forgotten, hidden and suppressed. The planet needs her warmth, nurture, creation and power to restore us to a rightful existence. One that coexists with nature as nature herself a reality that aligns with the moon, the stars and radiates with the power of the sun. Her melanin is recharged from every Ray as she connects with her Sistar’s to bring forth healing, harmony and balanc to the planet.. The divine feminine governs the spiritual realm and must have time to rest and restore, just like the seasons go through ebs and flows from hibernation to growth so does the divine feminine.

So she rests. Not just because she’s tired, but because rest is her rebellion and her revolution. Her pause is not laziness, it's building generational legacy. It’s how she ensures that her children, her community, and her future lineage inherit a healed woman, not a tired one continuing the cycle of survival. Rest becomes ritual. Restoration becomes requirement.

And in that rest, revelations come. Dreams are no longer deferred they become divine instructions. She listens. She journals. She takes spiritual baths with herbs her grandmother whispered about in her dreams. She lets her ancestors speak through the quiet. She lights her candles and speaks affirmations with a tongue dipped in honey and fire. She releases guilt for not doing it all because she knows *doing it all* is not her divine assignment. Being in alignment is.

She is no longer seeking permission. Her boundaries are blessed and backed by spirit. She doesn't over-explain. She doesn’t apologize for honoring her cycles. She reclaims her softness like a sacred prayer. Because what the world told her was weakness, was actually her womb wisdom calling her home.

The Divine Feminine doesn’t rush. She attracts. She knows that what’s meant for her doesn’t require burnout. It requires belief. So she gathers her spiritual tools, her oils, her cards, her intuition and she leads with intention. She mothers the future by first mothering herself.

And baby, the soft life? It ain't just about silk sheets and luxury lotions. It’s about sovereignty. It's about saying, *“I choose peace today, and I will not be moved.”* It's about divinely guided joy, self-worth, and holistic wholeness.

She no longer needs to prove anything. She just needs to simply *be*. And that *being* is the medicine. That *being* is the revolution. That *being* is the embodiment of the Divine Feminine walking fully in her power free, rested, powerful and whole.

She is not just surviving anymore. She is thriving.

And that, is sacred.
