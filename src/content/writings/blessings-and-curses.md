They told me my life was ruined. It’s hard to believe how reckless some people can be with their words. Words are very much spells that can cast the net of curses or blessings wide and may have long-term effects on a person. Without even knowing it, our words could change the course of a person‘s life or even worse, alter their perception of reality.

* * *

I was a young girl still in my teens hadn’t even hit my 20s yet and I was pregnant with my first daughter. I remember the day that my body sent subtle biological cues that something was different about me. Those flutters in the belly that felt like bubbles, I had a sneaking suspicion that I might be, but I still wasn’t sure. I knew for a fact I wasn’t ready to be. I was full of confusion, and the emotion that stood out the most was terrified. I had so many emotions popping into my mind that I could not conceive of how my life would be changed forever.

I can still remember that day like yesterday I was back in my mother‘s house, facing the first real trouble I had ever encountered in my young adult life, hell I had just become legally an adult by a couple of months and I was already facing legal trouble. Being ill-prepared for the streets, I fell for some fast-talking scam artist who got me into some shit. I had effectively ruined my life or at least I thought and felt I had, at the time I lost my first apartment, had to drop out of college and life seemed to be circling down the drain into a spiral of nowhere but a bottomless pit.

![Image by AnoukvanMarsbergen from Pixabay](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/5930f042-4bed-4170-b213-393f258bebbf_700x465.jpeg)

So I was sitting in my childhood bed and I called my best friend at the time and I told her that my body didn’t feel right, immediately she replied. “Do you think you’re pregnant?” Normally, I would say hell no but this time I said ”I don’t know I think so.” Because of the trouble that I was facing, I couldn’t leave the house so she did what a good best friend would do and got me the pregnancy test. I slowly walked into the bathroom like it was the last mile to death row and I peed on that stick. It wasn’t two minutes at all more like 15 seconds when I saw that = signaling that I was pregnant. Damn, this shit just got *REAL…*

Now remember, I just said I was in the most legal trouble I had ever been in my life. My mother was already quite disappointed in me and her emotions were just beginning to simmer down and now *this*. I had no husband, long time steady boyfriend just another kid from college and we were doing what college kids do and yes we had protected sex but as us grown folks know sometimes these boys and girls don’t know what they’re doing, and just like that I’m being called to bring forth another ancestor.

I didn’t find out until I was two months pregnant and I was trying to steer clear of my mother for the next two months, but was eventually starting to show, and my appetite was telling the whole story. I eventually told my mother when I was about four months pregnant. My mother was furious and ashamed because it didn’t fit her picture-perfect, one-size-fits-all narrative and with all the religion in our household, she acted as if this was an unnatural war against God. As my daughter began to grow inside of me, I started to get acclimated to the idea that I was gonna be somebody’s mother. Everyone in our circle that heard the news press from my mother’s lips, shamed me, and gave me this look and tone of doom and gloom, as if my life was over and everything was gonna go wrong for me.

What a horrible way to begin the existence of another spirit about to have a human experience. These types of experiences *definitely* did not help with the fear that I was feeling, but I would reflect on my friends who had children younger than me and they seemed to be getting along just fine so I held onto that hope. Eventually, my mother came around and started getting excited about the possibility of being a grandmother to her first granddaughter.

Nine months later, I became Isis’s portal of life. It was all hypothetical until the day I held my daughter in my arms. At the beginning of my pregnancy, certain people asked me if I was gonna keep it, *“it”* referred to the unknown gender of the life inside of me, and the thought of not accepting the assignment of life, never crossed my mind. I don’t say this to knock anybody’s decision, circumstance, or experience surrounding the termination of a pregnancy. I simply share my own experience and I’m so grateful that I took the baton passed to me. “Was my life harder?” I suppose it depends on who you ask. No one’s life is a path without challenges, and I don’t know anyone who hasn’t experienced their unique journey. With billions of people on the planet, not one single person has had an identical experience of life and the same applies to a young mother like me.

My life had its fair share of challenges, and I like to consider myself a late bloomer, but really that was because of the inner work and healing that was necessary for me on my path and my purpose. My girls never stopped me from accomplishing the many goals that people told me I wouldn’t be able to do because I had my girls early. It had nothing to do with having my children earlier, I raised my girls and we grew up together.

Eventually, their dad and I got back together and I had her sister a year and nine months apart, it didn’t work out between me and their dad, but I always have my girls. We had some hard times, good times, and everything in between and I wouldn’t trade a single day with anyone else’s days. Now they are young women and I’m a full-grown adult woman with wisdom, experience, grace, and dignity and I’m able to help them navigate the complex world of challenges that lie ahead in womanhood. My mother did not support me after having my girls and I had to learn certain aspects of being a woman from other people. My ancestors guided me heavily during these times and this experience credentialed me to be the mom to my daughters that my mother couldn’t be for me.

![Image by Christel SAGNIEZ from Pixabay](https://substack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com/public/images/b1f9954c-e53c-4e2f-81c4-c849fe37f74d_700x466.jpeg)

I was inspired to write this story as I sat reflecting the other day on my life, how amazing it is, and the blessings that are happening to me. Being the woman that I am today, I realize that I’m just beginning a new journey of life with wisdom and experience and I feel like life is really just starting and I get to grow into this phase with young adult women as they charter undiscovered territories of life that are already familiar to me. What a gift, if somebody had referenced the experience of parenthood like this 20 years ago, I may not have been so terrified. My oldest daughter is my bestie and we talk every day, sometimes I think she doesn’t listen to me, but I realize she drinks in every word that I say. If I offer a suggestion, I’ll hear her say oh I took your advice and I did this or that and it makes me smile on the inside and out. My youngest daughter is still feeling herself as these Gen Z‘s tend to do, she knows who to call and she just doesn’t know it yet but she’s my *Bestie* too!

The moral of this story is to keep your unfiltered, non-experience-backed, credentialless, negative perception to yourself. It’s damaging, particularly as it pertains to major life, decisions and situations of other people. The words that we say hold power and they can be either blessings or curses and no one should wield their power irresponsibly onto another human being, you are not the arbitrator of anyone’s life. Concern yourself more with your journey and less with others and always, always watch your mouth

Until the next time.

Peace
